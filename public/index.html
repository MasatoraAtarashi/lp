Spend a day in the Japan people actually live.

Most trips stack temples, anime streets, and the same photo spots. Everyday Japan is a small-group walk for visitors who want the weekday city: how a neighborhood works, what locals eat, and the texture of daily life — including the lived-in streets around Yoyogi Park.

You came for Japan. The itinerary handed you a checklist.

The famous places are easy. The ordinary city is not.

Guidebooks are excellent at shrines, crossings, and themed districts. They are quiet about a Tuesday: where people buy groceries, which cafe is actually used, how a park edge feels when nobody is performing for a camera.

"Local" often still means a stop, not a day.

A twenty-minute drop-in does not show you how a neighborhood spends an afternoon. The desire under the search is simpler: to be in the city people live, with enough time to notice it.

You want a person, not a loudspeaker.

English, a small group, and a host who lives here. Room to walk and sit. Not a bus, not a nightlife crawl, and not a lecture in front of a monument.

Sample walks, by neighborhood, era, and the shape of a day

These modules are the first season we want to test. They are not a fixed catalog, and they are not on sale yet. If this waitlist is real, these are the walks we open first.

Neighborhood

Yoyogi, on a weekday

The park is the landmark. The walk is a normal day around it: the lived-in edge of Yoyogi Park, a neighborhood street, a market or grocery stop, a cafe people actually use, and time with someone who lives nearby. Harajuku can sit in the background. It is not the point.

Era

One era, ordinary hours

A module flavored by a single period's daily routine — meals, shops, and the way a street still carries that rhythm. The era can change by departure. You are not collecting a shrine stamp or sitting through a museum talk. You are tracing how ordinary life felt, and what of it is still on the street.

A full afternoon

Markets, back streets, and dusk

Follow one neighborhood from an errand to evening. Where people buy food. What lunch looks like when nobody is visiting. How the block changes when the day ends. Built for travelers who would rather walk than queue.

Who it's for

  • First-time or returning visitors who are done collecting famous photo spots
  • People who like to walk, sit in a small cafe, and talk with a local in English
  • Travelers who want everyday life — including era-flavored routines — more than a theme-park version of Japan

Who should skip it

  • A packed highlights bus
  • A nightlife crawl
  • A photography sprint through Shibuya Crossing
